# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

Developers preparing for technical interviews: frontend, Tech Lead, Architect and full-stack roles. They come back to the page many times over weeks: they pick the next topic, open a video or course, and tick it off. The author is one of them; the page is public, and a circle of friends uses it together, seeing who has already finished each source.

## Product Purpose

A free, video-first study plan for technical interviews: 16 topics, and within each topic the sources ranked by depth (overview / intermediate / in depth). There is no importance ranking of topics; each reader puts the topics in their own order and hides the sources and topics that aren't relevant to them. A separate prep page holds what belongs to the interview itself rather than the study: a STAR story bank, questions for the interviewer, and a checklist for the last two weeks. Success: a visitor always knows what to study next and can see how far along they are.

## Positioning

Every source was opened and checked (YouTube oEmbed / HTTP), all content is free, video and full playlists/courses are preferred over reading, and the plan covers both Vue and React tracks plus AI across the SDLC and a full-stack topic.

## Operating Context

- Desktop for long study sessions; phone for browsing and ticking items between sessions.
- Without a login, progress lives only in the visitor's browser (localStorage). Friends can log in with GitHub: their progress is then kept in their account, follows them between devices, and is visible to every other friend (each card shows who already finished it). Open pages update live, without a reload, over a WebSocket. There is no private option; this is for friends.
- Stored per account: GitHub id, login, name and avatar, which sources were finished and when, and the reader's layout (topic order, hidden topics and sources). The layout is private: friends see only what was finished. No email, password or IP address.
- Shared as a link (Open Graph preview image at `og-image.png`).

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Two static HTML pages generated by `build.mjs`, no framework, no extra build step: the plan (`index.html`, from `README.md` with `template.html`) and the interview prep page (`prep.html`, from `interview-prep.md` with `prep-template.html`, sharing the plan's styles). GitHub Pages serves the repo root.
- A small accounts API beside it: a Cloudflare Worker with a D1 database in `api/` (GitHub OAuth login, each person's progress, everyone's finished sources). It is deployed separately (`api/README.md`); the page talks to it with a bearer token and works fully without it.
- Features that must keep working: checkboxes with persisted progress, overall and per-topic progress, filters (depth, video only, hide done, show hidden), Vue/React tabs, the cabinet's drawers as table of contents, internal anchors, the "how to use" and "tool status" dialogs (also opened by `#how` / `#status`), arranging the topics (drag and up/down) and hiding sources and topics (out of every count), the prep page's own checklist (in the browser only), the guest experience with no login, and for logged-in friends: sync of progress and layout between devices and friends' avatars on the cards.
- Content is Hebrew, RTL; source titles and links are English.
- Must work well on phone and support dark mode (system preference).
- Text files use CRLF.
- Counts (topics, sources, playlist items, sources per topic) are never written by hand: `node build.mjs` computes them from the README, fills the page's meta tags and the OG image (rendering `og-image.png` again when a number changes), and prints them.

## Evidence on Hand

- The plan content itself (`README.md`), with verified source dates and lengths.
- No testimonials, user counts or endorsements exist; do not invent any.

## Product Principles

1. The next thing to study is always obvious.
2. Progress is visible and motivating, but never in the way of the content.
3. Depth is the core ranking and must read at a glance; the order of topics is the reader's own.
4. Free and verified: no claims beyond what was checked.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Keep WCAG 2.2 AA as the floor: keyboard access for tabs, filters and checkboxes, visible focus, sufficient contrast in both themes, reduced motion respected.
